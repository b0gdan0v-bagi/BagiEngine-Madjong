# Table/ — the scene as pure functions

Everything here is plain C++ over the model, a `TableView` or a `UIStrings&`: no scene, no renderer, no engine services, so the tests need none. The components in `../Components/` draw and publish what these functions return.

## Rules

- **No peeking.** Hints, marks and advice read only `MakeTableView(hand, viewer)` or public data (open melds, the pile, declarations, `Moves()`), never another seat's hand or the live wall. Tests compare models that differ only in hidden data.
- **Words:** one key with `{placeholders}` per sentence; word order belongs to the translation, never pieces joined in code. Rules pages never retype a number or a button word: numbers are placeholders filled from the rule constants, button words come from `WordOf` (`RulesTextTest` fails on a digit outside a placeholder).

## The table layout

`MakeTableLayout(TableInput[, viewWidth, viewHeight])` → `TableLayout { viewer, shape, slots, zones }`; a `TileSlot { rect, role (HandTile | MeldTile | BonusTile | DiscardTile | WallStack), seat, index, part, claimEdge, rotation }`. The layout knows no tile; the component maps `index` to tiles.

- **Two shapes** (`TableShape`). Landscape: design 1280 x 720 (`kViewWidth` / `kViewHeight`, repeated in `config/SceneConfig.xml`). Portrait: 720 x 1280 (`kPortraitViewWidth` / `kPortraitViewHeight`, the scene's `viewVariants` entry for `orientation:portrait`: an output at least as high as wide, a square too). The rule is one function, `ShapeOfView(w, h)` (`ShapeOfOutput` for an output; a size that is not positive is landscape), and `MahjongSceneFilesTest` holds the scene file to it. The layout is built in the design of the shape of the view it is given and fitted into that view (`LayoutScale`, uniform, centred), so a landscape view gives today's layout byte for byte (the test keeps a stored copy of its numbers).
- **The portrait table** (`TableLayout.cpp`, "portrait"): the top seat as in landscape; under it a band of the three opponents' discard blocks with the side seats' hand, meld and bonus columns at the edges; a row of three plates; the wall ring; the viewer's discards (one and a half times the landscape tile); then free ground, which the canvases of the table use (`config/nodes/`); the viewer's plate, bonus row, meld row and the hand at the foot. **The viewer's hand is one row of 14 tiles of 48 x 64** across the view resting on the rim; on a 390 pt phone that tile is about 26 pt, so the touch rule (`UsesTapToSelect`) is what makes it playable. Portrait has no phone profile: `TableProfileFor` gives the desktop one for a portrait view and the layout ignores `profile`. A two-row hand is the owner's call after a look at a phone.
- The touch rules (`FitScale`, `BarUnderTable`, `NeedsCompactActions`, `TableProfileFor`, `UsesTapToSelect`) take the view's size; the action buttons are 56 high in landscape and 68 in portrait (`ActionButtonHeightFor`: the portrait view is 720 wide, so a phone scales it by about 0.5).
- The viewer is at the bottom; `SeatPosition(viewer, seat)` 0 bottom, 1 right, 2 top, 3 left.
- **The side seats are turned.** Every tile of the seats at screen positions 1 (right) and 3 (left) has `rotation` 270 / 90 (degrees clockwise on the screen, `kRightSeatRotation` / `kLeftSeatRotation`), so each reads upright for the player who sits there; the top seat's tiles and the wall stay upright (the owner's decision). `rect` is always the screen rectangle (what the eye sees; marks, hit tests and `claimEdge` use it), wider than high for a turned slot; `UprightRect(rect, rotation)` is the unturned rectangle a draw takes, with the turn about the centre of `rect`. A side strip runs top to bottom for both seats with the pitch of the turned tile; the discard block of a side seat is the block turned (a row runs the way the seat reads it, left top to bottom, right bottom to top, row 0 at the outer side, the next row toward the middle; squeezed rows squeeze along the horizontal). The columns are the squeeze of the table: the right discard block, the `Hint` label of `MahjongActions.xml`, the side seat plates, the score table (x 104..1176) and the HUD's move line all depend on the numbers in `TableLayout.cpp`, next to the `static_assert`s; the side hands are smaller backs (`kSideHandTile*`) for that reason.
- Discards: a block per seat, 6 per row, extra rows squeezed (a zone never grows). The wall: a ring of `ceil(Remaining() / 2)` stacks filled clockwise (a simplification; the physical break is not tracked).
- Every zone holds the most the rules allow (14 concealed, four kongs, 8 bonus tiles, the 144-tile wall); no slot leaves the view and no zone overlaps another — `TableLayoutTest` holds this for the worst-case table.
- **The phone profile** (landscape only; `TableInput::profile`, recorded in `TableLayout::profile`; default desktop, byte-identical to the table without it). `TableProfileFor(output size, density, minTouchSize)` gives it to a window where the desktop hand tile (`kViewerHandTileW` x fit / density) is under the touch minimum, with a hand tile just wide enough to reach it, at most `kPhoneHandTileMaxW` (a window too small for the minimum gets the maximum); the input is clamped to `kViewerHandTileW..kPhoneHandTileMaxW`. Only the viewer's side changes: the 14 hand tiles are one row spread over the view inside the rim and the rack padding, resting on the rim (the desktop hand ends there too), so they never touch; the bonus tiles and melds are one row above the tallest hand tile, right of the bottom plate; the bottom discard block is lower and shorter (its rows squeeze sooner, `DiscardRowPitch`); the bottom plate moves up to end where the tallest hand tile starts (its label in `MahjongSeatPlates.xml` stays on it). The wall ring, the centre panel, the three other seats and plates stay exactly; the numbers and their `static_assert`s are in `TableLayout.cpp`, "phone profile". A canvas on the table in a phone window must be checked against that layout, not the desktop one.
- `zones`: the discard blocks, the wall ring, the four seat plates, `centerPanel` (the inside of the wall ring, the ring less one stack, the same for every viewer: holds no slot). `claimEdge` marks the claimed tile of an open meld and the side facing the seat it came from. `BoundsOfMeld`, `HitHandTile`: forgiving, the region is the viewer's hand strip (top of the tiles to the bottom of the view, first tile's left edge to the last tile's right edge, not raised with a selected tile); the tile nearest in x wins, the later on overlap or a tie. The bonus row above and the melds to the right are outside it.
