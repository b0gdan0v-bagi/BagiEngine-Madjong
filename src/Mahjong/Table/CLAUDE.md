# Table/ — the scene as pure functions

Everything here is plain C++ over the model, a `TableView` or a `UIStrings&`: no scene, no renderer, no engine services, so the tests need none (one exception: `MakeTableLayout` without a spec logs one `LOG_ERROR` when no spec is set, see "The table layout"; the XML reader takes a pugixml node and knows no `ConfigManager`). The components in `../Components/` draw and publish what these functions return.

## Rules

- **No peeking.** Hints, marks and advice read only `MakeTableView(hand, viewer)` or public data (open melds, the pile, declarations, `Moves()`), never another seat's hand or the live wall. Tests compare models that differ only in hidden data.
- **Words:** one key with `{placeholders}` per sentence; word order belongs to the translation, never pieces joined in code. Rules pages never retype a number or a button word: numbers are placeholders filled from the rule constants, button words come from `WordOf` (`RulesTextTest` fails on a digit outside a placeholder).

## The table layout

`MakeTableLayout(TableInput[, viewWidth, viewHeight])` → `TableLayout { viewer, shape, slots, zones }`; a `TileSlot { rect, role (HandTile | MeldTile | BonusTile | DiscardTile | WallStack), seat, index, part, claimEdge, rotation }`. The layout knows no tile; the component maps `index` to tiles.

- **The zones are data.** `config/TableLayout.xml` (how to edit it: `config/CLAUDE.md`) is read into a `TableLayoutSpec` (`TableLayoutSpec.h`, plain values; `TableLayoutSpecXml.h` parses with errors that name the shape, the seat and the attribute): per shape and screen position the zones of the hand, melds, bonus tiles, discards and the plate, the wall ring, the phone override of the viewer's side, and Wide as `like` Landscape with an inset. A zone is a rectangle as **fractions of the design view of its shape**, snapped to a quarter unit by edges (`SnapZone`), so the float noise of a fraction never reaches a tile. **No tile size is written down:** `TableSolver.h` takes the largest integer width `w` (height `floor(w * 4 / 3)`, the atlas proportions) that fits the zone for what the rules allow it to hold (14 concealed tiles, four kongs with `kMeldGap` between melds, 8 bonus tiles, a discard block of `rows` rows of `kDiscardsPerRow`, the ring of `kWallRingColumns` x `kWallRingRows` square stacks; a side seat's tile is turned, so its zone's width must hold the tile's height). So a zone is never too small for its tiles, a larger one gives a larger tile, and `minTileW` on a zone refuses a file that would solve smaller. A discard pile longer than its rows squeezes inside the block (a zone never grows).
- **Who builds, who checks.** `MakeTableLayout(input, spec, w, h)` is the pure builder over the solved zones; the overload without a spec reads `ActiveTableLayoutSpec()` (`SetActiveTableLayoutSpec`, main thread only, the caller keeps the spec alive). With none set the layout has its shape and no slots and the first such call logs one `LOG_ERROR`: a missing spec is a wiring mistake the player would otherwise see only as an empty table. The game sets it from the config `TableLayout` and polls the file (`Components/CLAUDE.md`); the gtest binary sets the shipped file from the source tree (`ShippedTableLayout.h`). `CheckTableLayoutSpec(spec)` builds the worst table for every shape and viewer (and the phone profile at the least, a middle and the greatest width) and returns one message per problem: a slot outside the view, zones overlapping, a plate on a slot or nearer than `kPlatePadding` to a block or the ring, a tile under its `minTileW`, a zone too small, the viewer's hand tile under `kViewerHandTileW`, the widest phone tile other than `kPhoneHandTileMaxW`. `OfferTableLayoutSpec(node)` (reader + check + compare) applies a file only when it reads, is sound and differs, so a bad edit leaves the last good spec.
- **Named numbers that stay in code**, each held to the file by a test: the view sizes, `kViewerHandTileW/H` (the touch rules use them), `kPhoneHandTileMaxW`, `kSeatPlateW/H` (`MahjongSeatPlates.xml` repeats them), `kMax*`, `kDiscardsPerRow`, `kDiscardRowCapacity`, `kWallRing*`, `kMeldGap`. **Clearances the scene files rely on** (the right discard block clear of the `Hint` label of `MahjongActions.xml`, the top hand right of the `WaitPanel` of `MahjongHints.xml`, the side bonus columns below the move line of `MahjongHud.xml`, the viewer's discard block right of the `SelectedTile` label of `MahjongHud.xml` (`kSelectedTileRight`), the top bonus row left of the `Rules` button of `MahjongHudMenu.xml`) are kept by the zones and named in the comments of the file; `TheSceneClearancesHold` fails when a zone crosses one. The node files keep absolute numbers until the zones are published as named regions.
- **Four shapes** (`TableShape`), each a view of the scene with a design size and its own `viewVariants` entry in `config/SceneConfig.xml`. Landscape: 1280 x 720 (`kViewWidth` / `kViewHeight`; 16:9, 16:10 and what lies between). Portrait: 720 x 1280 (`orientation:portrait`: an output at least as high as wide, a square too). Narrow: 1080 x 768 (`orientation:landscape;maxAspect:1.55`: 4:3, 3:2, 5:4). Wide: 1560 x 720 (`minAspect:2`: 21:9, 32:9, every landscape phone). The rule is one function, `ShapeOfView(w, h)` (`ShapeOfOutput` for an output; a size that is not positive is landscape; `kNarrowMaxAspect`, `kWideMinAspect`), and `MahjongSceneFilesTest` holds the scene file to it; a canvas variant on the table repeats the `when` text of its view (a canvas sees its bounds, which are the view), so the conditions exclude each other. The layout is built in the design of the shape of the view it is given and fitted into that view (`LayoutScale`, uniform, centred), so a landscape view gets the landscape design unchanged. **Wide is the landscape geometry shifted by `kWideWingWidth` (140) on each side, not a second geometry** (`TableLayoutTest` holds "wide = landscape shifted"): the table is as high as before, so the tiles do not grow, and the wings are free ground; the phone profile works in it. **Narrow** is the landscape table's side columns, discards, wall ring and centre panel in 1080 x 768 with the viewer's side as the phone profile lays it out (the 14 tiles one row of the desktop size, the bonus tiles and melds in a row above it); the 48 units of height pay for that row; its side columns run from under the move line to above the bottom left buttons of `HudMenu` (the same on both sides), which is what limits their tile. Portrait and narrow ignore the profile.
- **The portrait table** (`Portrait` in the file): the top seat a margin below the edge (a phone's status bar); under it a band of the three opponents' discard blocks with the side seats' hand and meld columns at the edges (they start under the top hand, y 84, to be as long as in landscape); a row of three plates, each side plate beside its seat's bonus column (the band has no width for a third column: two columns, two side blocks and the top block fill the 720 units); the wall ring; the viewer's discards (one and a half times the landscape tile, five rows); every opponent's tiles are no smaller than in landscape (`TableLayoutTest` holds it); then free ground, which the canvases of the table use (`config/nodes/`); the viewer's plate, bonus row, meld row and the hand at the foot. **The viewer's hand is one row of 14 tiles of the desktop size** (`kViewerHandTileW`), spread across the view and resting on the rim; on a 390 pt phone that tile is about 26 pt, so the touch rule (`UsesTapToSelect`) is what makes it playable. Portrait has no phone profile: `TableProfileFor` gives the desktop one for a portrait or narrow view and the layout ignores `profile`. A two-row hand is the owner's call after a look at a phone.
- The touch rules (`FitScale`, `BarUnderTable`, `NeedsCompactActions`, `TableProfileFor`, `UsesTapToSelect`) take the view's size; the action buttons are 56 high in every landscape-like shape and 68 in portrait (`ActionButtonHeightFor`: the portrait view is 720 wide, so a phone scales it by about 0.5).
- The viewer is at the bottom; `SeatPosition(viewer, seat)` 0 bottom, 1 right, 2 top, 3 left.
- **The side seats are turned.** Every tile of the seats at screen positions 1 (right) and 3 (left) has `rotation` 270 / 90 (degrees clockwise on the screen, `kRightSeatRotation` / `kLeftSeatRotation`), so each reads upright for the player who sits there; the top seat's tiles and the wall stay upright (the owner's decision). `rect` is always the screen rectangle (what the eye sees; marks, hit tests and `claimEdge` use it), wider than high for a turned slot; `UprightRect(rect, rotation)` is the unturned rectangle a draw takes, with the turn about the centre of `rect`. A side strip runs top to bottom for both seats with the pitch of the turned tile; the discard block of a side seat is the block turned (a row runs the way the seat reads it, left top to bottom, right bottom to top, row 0 at the outer side, the next row toward the middle; squeezed rows squeeze along the horizontal). The side columns are the squeeze of the table: the right discard block, the `Hint` label, the side seat plates, the score table and the HUD's move line all depend on where the side zones lie, so the side hands are smaller backs than the top one (their zones are narrower). The top hand is not centred: it starts right of the hints' `WaitPanel`, and the top seat's bonus tiles lie right of its melds, left of the `Rules` button.
- Discards: a block per seat, 6 per row, extra rows squeezed (a zone never grows). The wall: a ring of `ceil(Remaining() / 2)` stacks filled clockwise (a simplification; the physical break is not tracked).
- Every zone holds the most the rules allow (14 concealed, four kongs, 8 bonus tiles, the 144-tile wall); no slot leaves the view and no zone overlaps another — `TableLayoutTest` holds this for the worst-case table.
- **The phone profile** (the landscape and wide shapes only; `TableInput::profile`, recorded in `TableLayout::profile`; default desktop, byte-identical to the table without it). `TableProfileFor(output size, density, minTouchSize)` gives it to a window where the desktop hand tile (`kViewerHandTileW` x fit / density) is under the touch minimum, with a hand tile just wide enough to reach it, at most `kPhoneHandTileMaxW` (a window too small for the minimum gets the maximum); the input is clamped to `kViewerHandTileW..kPhoneHandTileMaxW`. Only the viewer's side changes: the 14 hand tiles are one row side by side (as on the desktop, the owner's call) from the left inside the rim and the rack padding, resting on the rim (the desktop hand ends there too); the bonus tiles and melds are one row above the tallest hand tile, right of the bottom plate; the bottom discard block is lower and shorter (`rows="3"`: its rows squeeze sooner); the bottom plate moves up to end where the tallest hand tile starts (its label in `MahjongSeatPlates.xml` stays on it). The wall ring, the centre panel, the three other seats and plates stay exactly; its zones are the `Phone` element of the file. A canvas on the table in a phone window must be checked against that layout, not the desktop one.
- `zones`: the discard blocks, the wall ring, the four seat plates, `centerPanel` (the inside of the wall ring, the ring less one stack, the same for every viewer: holds no slot). `claimEdge` marks the claimed tile of an open meld and the side facing the seat it came from. `BoundsOfMeld`, `HitHandTile`: forgiving, the region is the viewer's hand strip (top of the tiles to the bottom of the view, first tile's left edge to the last tile's right edge, not raised with a selected tile); the tile nearest in x wins, the later on overlap or a tie. The bonus row above and the melds to the right are outside it.
